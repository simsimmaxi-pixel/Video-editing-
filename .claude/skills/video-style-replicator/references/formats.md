# Format priors

What usually matters for each format, so you know which lenses to open and what to look for. These are
starting expectations, not rules: the reference's own measurements always win, and a creator's style is
often defined by how it departs from its format.

| Format | Usually defined by | Lenses beyond CD + editor | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical short (TikTok/Reels/Shorts, 7-60 s) | A hook in the first 1-3 s, dense pacing, music or trend audio, native-looking text | music, graphics | platform UI/watermarks, safe zones, loop-ability of the ending |
| Event recap / montage | Burst/hold rhythm, people-first holds, venue establishing shots, section-level music lock | music, colourist (mixed cameras) | in-camera whips and spins, bookend endings |
| YouTube talking head / explainer | Speech-led jump cuts, punch-ins, B-roll cadence, a hook that proves the title, chapters | long-form, sound, graphics | dead-air removal, room tone, music ducking, end screen zone |
| Documentary / video essay | VO over archival/B-roll, slow holds, score-driven arcs, text cards | long-form, music, colourist, sound | J/L cuts, open loops, dissolves, letterboxing |
| Vlog | Handheld or selfie A-roll, quick B-roll inserts, casual captions, music bed changes per scene | long-form, sound | ambience, mixed cameras, time-lapse/speed shots |
| Trailer / cinematic | Three-act energy (setup → rise → montage climax → title), music hits locked to cuts, risers, black-frame beats | music, sound, colourist, graphics | dips to black, title cards, letterbox, teal-orange or matte looks |
| Music video | Cuts on the beat grid, performance vs story intercut, effects/treatments, repeated motifs | music, colourist | beat-synced cutting (check the sync tests), speed ramps, flash frames |
| Product ad / UGC ad | A problem-solution arc in 15-30 s, product hero holds, on-screen claims, CTA end card | graphics, sound | readable claims, brand colours, end card timing |

## Aspect and delivery defaults (confirm with the user)
- Vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps; YouTube 1920x1080 (or 3840x2160), 24/25/30 fps matching the footage; cinematic 2.39:1
  is usually letterboxed inside 16:9.
- Loudness: about -14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ -1 dBTP, unless the reference measures otherwise and the user wants a match.
- Keep the frame rate of the reference unless the footage forces otherwise. 60 fps sources conform fine to 30 fps (the engine does it).
