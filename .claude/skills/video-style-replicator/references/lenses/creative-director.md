# Lens: Creative director

Job: say what the video is *for* and why it works, so every later decision serves that. This lens sets the
priorities that the other lenses trade off against.

## Questions to answer (2-6 lines each in the blueprint)
1. **Purpose and audience.** Who is it for, and what should they feel or do afterwards (FOMO, trust, laugh,
   buy, subscribe, learn)? Which platform, and how does it get watched (sound-off feed, TV, lean-in desktop)?
2. **The promise.** What does the first 1-3 s (short-form) or 10-30 s (long-form) promise, and where is it paid off?
3. **Emotional arc.** Map the energy over time using `summary.md` (rms curve, block structure, pacing curve):
   flat-high montage, build-to-payoff, three-act, or wave (burst/breathe/burst). Name the peaks.
4. **Tone in three words.** E.g. "raw, communal, hype" or "calm, premium, precise". These words judge every choice later.
5. **What makes it work.** The two or three things that, if missing, would make the copy feel wrong.
   This is the fidelity priority list. Everything else is negotiable.
6. **Motifs and signatures.** Recurring visual or audio ideas: a bookend location, a repeated framing,
   a colour, a sound, a running gag.
7. **What not to copy.** Platform artifacts, the creator's identity (their face, handle or brand), anything copyrighted
   that the user doesn't own (their music, logos), and accidents (a blurry frame isn't a style).

## Fidelity vs adaptation
- "Recreate this" means matching the grammar closely: pacing distribution, structure fractions, transition mix,
  look stats, overlay placement. Deviate only where the new material forces it, and say where.
- "In the spirit of" means keeping items 3-6 and adapting the numbers to the new footage and music.
- New footage rarely contains the reference's exact moments. Find the *functional equivalents*: a held
  human-reaction shot can stand in for any other held human-reaction shot. Match by what a shot is for
  (establish, react, act, reveal, breathe), not by subject.
- The new music has its own shape. Map the reference's structure onto the music's sections, not onto clock time.

## Deciding between conflicting rules
Walter Murch's ranking (In the Blink of an Eye): emotion (~51%) > story (~23%) > rhythm (~10%) > eye-trace (~7%) >
screen planarity (~5%) > spatial continuity (~4%). The weights are tongue-in-cheek, but the order is useful.
If a technically perfect cut kills the moment, keep the moment.

## Series and variants
For a run of posts in one style: keep the grammar and tone words identical, and vary the hook, the hero moments,
the music window and the ending. Let the engine's "overlaps a moment already used" warning, applied across
the series' edit files, stop the posts from feeling recycled.
